## Overview

A suite of real-world games designed to encourage altruistic behaviour among strangers. Players are rewarded with points for actions that benefit the broader commons — overcoming the natural human tendency toward self-interest. The suite currently comprises three games:

- **Spendium** — rewards ethical purchases
- **Polium** — rewards ethical voting
- **Humanium** — rewards ethical life choices

All games share a common loop: surveys shape ratings, ratings drive interest, and interest drives real-world actions — purchases, votes, life choices. Players can enter the loop at any point and earn points for their participation.

## Philosophical Foundation

### The Tragedy of the Commons

The games are grounded in the work of **Elinor Ostrom** (1933–2012), Nobel laureate in Economics. Ostrom challenged the prevailing assumption that shared resources are inevitably over-exploited by self-interested individuals — the so-called "tragedy of the commons." She demonstrated through fieldwork across multiple continents that communities *can* sustainably manage shared resources, provided they develop appropriate institutional structures built on trust, monitoring, and collective rule-making.

Her eight design principles for sustainable commons management — including clearly defined boundaries, collective rule-making, graduated sanctions, and nested governance — serve as an intellectual framework for this game suite.

### Definition of "Ethical"

Throughout this document, **ethical** means behaviour that:

- Overcomes the default human tendency toward narrow self-interest
- Contributes to a shared commons — environmental, social, or economic
- Benefits the broadest possible group, with the ultimate goal being the **long-term flourishing of all humanity**
- Cannot be co-opted by any subgroup to serve their own ends at the expense of others — **no smaller group can be allowed to dominate**

This is a demanding definition. The group being benefited can be flexibly defined (local community, nation, species, biosphere), but the direction must always be toward greater inclusivity and long-term sustainability, never toward the entrenchment of a narrower interest.

## The Organisation: Human Flourishing (HF)

Human Flourishing (HF) is the organisation that governs the game suite. It serves two roles: it operates the games directly, and it sets and upholds the constitutional rules that govern all activity within the ecosystem. The long-term goal is for HF to become fully self-governing — robust to the loss of any founding individual, and capable of anticipating and responding to events and developments that no single person could foresee.

This self-governing ambition is itself Ostromian: Ostrom demonstrated that institutions can be designed to manage shared resources sustainably without depending on any central authority, provided they have the right structure of rules, monitoring, and collective decision-making.

### The Constitution

All activity of Human Flourishing is governed by four constitutional articles. They are not strictly hierarchical — they are interlocking principles that together define the decision space. Where they appear to conflict, the tension is resolved by the HF Constitutional Court.

The four articles are **unamendable**. They are not policies. They are axioms — the things HF believes to be true regardless of circumstance. No process, however large the majority, can remove or weaken them. The constitution can grow — new articles may be added — but what is already here cannot be taken away.

Each article is accompanied by canonical commentary that records the founder's original intent. This commentary is not itself binding in the way the article is, but it anchors the Court's interpretation when applying the articles to circumstances the founders could not have foreseen.

---

**Article 1: Make the world a wonderful place to be for everyone, forever.**

*This is the only thing that matters. Everything else in this constitution — every game, every criterion, every governance process — exists in service of this single idea. When in doubt about any decision, come back here. Not "is this legal?" Not "did we follow the process?" But: does this make the world more wonderful for more people, over a longer time horizon? If the honest answer is no, stop.*

*"Everyone" means everyone. Not our members, not our players, not people who share our values. Everyone. "Forever" means we are not permitted to trade the future for the present. A decision that makes things better today at the cost of tomorrow fails this article.*

*This article asks for the greatest flourishing of the greatest number, not the unconstrained flourishing of any one person. My maximum cannot come at the cost of yours. Where individual maximums conflict, we seek the highest total — and that calculation always, without exception, includes everyone.*

---

**Article 2: Profound human connections.**

*We chose "profound" deliberately. Shallow connection — a like, a follow, a transaction — is not what this article protects. We mean the kind of connection that changes people. That makes strangers into neighbours. That makes opponents into collaborators. That reminds us that the person on the other side of a political disagreement is still a person.*

*HF operates in the physical world because that is where real connection happens. A conversation across a shop counter, a vote cast in a shared civic space, a purchase that keeps a local business alive — these matter more than any digital interaction, however convenient. The app is a tool. The game is a reason to engage. But the point is always the moment when two human beings, who might never have spoken otherwise, find themselves on the same side of something that matters. Virtual connection can point toward that moment. It cannot replace it. When this organisation faces a choice between what is easy online and what is meaningful in person, it must always choose the physical world.*

---

**Article 3: The weight of evidence wins.**

*This article exists to prevent HF from becoming what it was designed to fight: an institution that serves its own beliefs rather than the truth. We do not get to decide in advance what the evidence will show. We do not get to ignore evidence that is inconvenient. We do not get to favour the evidence that confirms what we already think.*

*When evidence conflicts with current practice — including practices this organisation has invested in, built around, or publicly committed to — the evidence wins. This is uncomfortable by design. An organisation that changes its mind when the evidence demands it is one that can be trusted. One that doesn't, cannot.*

*A warning: almost everyone believes they are good at following evidence. Almost everyone is wrong. The reliable signal that you are actually doing it is discomfort — the specific discomfort of being shown that something you believed, built, or defended is not what you thought it was. If following the evidence never makes you uncomfortable, you are not following the evidence. You are following your existing beliefs and calling them evidence. This article demands more than that. It demands the kind of intellectual honesty that costs something.*

---

**Article 4: Purpose is sovereign over process.**

*Institutions fail when the people running them mistake the rules for the point. Rules are tools. They exist to serve the purpose, not to replace it. This article is here for the moment when someone in HF — a justice, an administrator, a founder — finds themselves about to do something that is technically correct but obviously wrong.*

*Stop. Ask the question this article requires: stripped of the justification, the precedent, the procedure — does this serve all of humanity, or does it serve the people making the decision? History has a way of answering that question eventually. This article requires us to answer it first, while there is still time to choose differently.*

### The Constitutional Court

The Constitutional Court is the final arbiter of what is and is not constitutional within HF. It interprets the four articles, adjudicates disputes, reviews adopted criteria on referral, and gatekeeps the addition of new constitutional articles. It is independent of day-to-day operations and of the criteria governance process.

The Court has **5 justices**.

#### Seat 1 — The Founder's Seat

The founder holds a permanent seat on the Constitutional Court. This is an acknowledged concentration of power, justified by the founding role and stated explicitly rather than obscured.

The founder must at all times have a designated successor to this seat on public record, lodged with the Court. The designation may be changed at any time. The successor must be a human individual — corporations, organisations, and AI systems are ineligible. On the founder's death or incapacitation, the designated successor assumes Seat 1 immediately. That successor must in turn designate their own successor, and so on in perpetuity.

If the founder dies or becomes incapacitated without a valid successor designation on record, Seat 1 remains vacant until the remaining Court justices unanimously agree on a successor. The Court operates with 4 justices during any vacancy on Seat 1.

This seat is the only one that does not rotate. It is the institutional memory and continuity of HF's founding intent.

#### Seat 2 — The Tenure Seat

Reserved for the longest-tenured active member in good standing who has not previously served on the Court. Tenure is measured by continuous membership — years of uninterrupted annual fee payment. The longest continuous member who is eligible and willing holds this seat for a **2-year term**. Once served, cannot serve again.

If the longest-tenured member declines, the seat passes to the next longest-tenured eligible member, and so on. If no eligible member is willing to serve, Seat 2 remains vacant. The Court operates with the remaining justices during any vacancy. There is no obligation to serve.

#### Seats 3, 4, and 5 — Community Elected Seats

These three seats are filled by community election. Any member in good standing may nominate themselves or another member. Confirmation requires a **60% supermajority** of participating members with a minimum **10% of active members** participating.

**Terms:** staggered **4-year terms**. To establish the stagger from day one, the three founding community justices draw lots for 2-year, 3-year, and 4-year initial terms. All subsequent terms are 4 years. One seat turns over approximately every 18 months — no single election cycle can capture all three seats simultaneously.

**Once served, cannot serve again.**

**Vacancy mid-term:** the remaining Court members nominate a temporary justice by unanimous agreement to serve until the next scheduled election. If the Court cannot agree unanimously, the Tenure Seat holder casts the deciding vote. If Seat 2 is also vacant, the founder or their successor casts the deciding vote.

#### Court Processes

**Quorum:** 3 of 5 justices must participate in any decision. If vacancies reduce the Court below 5, quorum is a simple majority of sitting justices.

**Majority:** decisions require a majority of participating justices. No abstentions permitted — a justice who participates must vote.

**Criteria referral review:** when a passed criterion is referred by a member in good standing, the Court must respond within 30 days. Failure to respond is treated as no constitutional objection. The Court may: uphold the criterion (no action), strike it down (criterion is void), or remand it to the membership with specific concerns for revision.

**Recusal:** a justice with a direct conflict of interest must recuse themselves. A recused justice is replaced for that matter by the next longest-tenured eligible non-serving member.

**Transparency:** all Court decisions, including reasoning, are published publicly in the forum. Dissenting opinions may be published. No private decisions.

**Precedent:** Court decisions accumulate as binding precedent on future Courts. A future Court may revisit precedent but must explicitly address and provide reasoning for any departure from it.

#### Removal of a Justice

A justice may be removed by a **75% supermajority** of participating members with a minimum **20% participation threshold**.

Seat 1 — the founder's seat — cannot be removed by member vote. It can only be vacated by the founder or their designated successor stepping down, or by death or incapacitation triggering succession.

A removed justice may not serve again.

#### Adding a New Constitutional Article

The four existing articles are unamendable. The constitution can grow — new articles may be added when humanity encounters situations requiring new foundational principles. New articles, once ratified, are themselves immediately unamendable.

**Process:**

1. **Proposal** — any member in good standing may propose a new constitutional article in the forum
2. **Mandatory deliberation** — minimum **90 days** from first proposal before any vote
3. **Court endorsement** — requires **at least 4 of 5 Court justices**. The Court must verify the proposed article does not contradict or weaken any existing article.
4. **Member ratification** — requires **75% of participating members** with a minimum **20% of active members** participating
5. **Adoption** — if ratified, the new article is immediately unamendable

### Governance

HF governance is modelled loosely on a constitutional democracy. The constitution and the Constitutional Court sit above all other rules and processes. The criteria governance process, the membership, and the games all operate beneath them.

## Core Gameplay Loop

All three games share a single loop, arranged in a circle. A player can enter at any point — there is no required starting point and no end.

**1. Survey → Rating**
Players submit yes/no survey responses about a real-world entity — a store, a political candidate, a life choice. Each response contributes to that entity's ethics rating. The rating is a continuously updated, community-generated assessment of how well that entity serves the goals of human flourishing. Anyone can survey anything at any time.

**2. Rating → Interest**
Ratings are visible to everyone. A high-rated candidate attracts attention and declared support. A high-rated store attracts ethical shoppers. A low rating drives people away. A blacklisted candidate — one who endorsed HF, won an election, and then betrayed that commitment — is permanently marked as having broken trust with the community.

**3. Interest → Action**
Interest drives real-world behaviour. Players spend money at higher-rated brands. Players vote for — and publicly declare they will vote for — higher-rated candidates. Actions earn points, which rewards the player and reinforces the loop. Survey submissions also earn points directly — participation at every stage of the loop is rewarded, not just the final action.

**Entering the loop**
A new player can begin anywhere:
- They can **survey** a candidate or store they already know something about
- They can **browse ratings** to find a good place to shop or a good candidate to support, then act
- They can **take an action first** — make a purchase or declare a vote — and be prompted to survey afterward

The loop is self-reinforcing and self-correcting. More surveys produce more accurate ratings. More accurate ratings drive better actions. Better actions generate more surveys. Over time the system becomes a living, evidence-based guide to ethical behaviour in the real world — one that no single person designed or controls, and that survives and adapts beyond any individual founder.

### Rating Trend Indicator

Every subject profile — candidate, brand, product — displays a **rating trend indicator** alongside the current rating. The trend indicator shows whether the rating has been rising, falling, or flat over the past 24 months.

**Two components:**

- **Sparkline** — a small inline chart showing the rating curve over the past 24 months. Gives players the full picture at a glance.
- **Arrow** — a simple up/down/flat summary for players who want a quick signal.

The default trend window is **24 months**, configurable.

**Why this matters:** a subject at 60% with a strong upward trend over 24 months is a fundamentally different proposition from a subject at 60% that has been flat for two years, or one that was at 75% eighteen months ago. The current rating tells players where a subject is. The trend tells them where it is going — without HF making any editorial judgment about intentions, promises, or journeys. The data speaks for itself.

This is also the mechanism by which genuine corporate or political improvement is recognised. A corporation that actually changes its behaviour will see its rating rise and its trend indicator turn upward. No promises required. No multi-year audit agreements. Just evidence of actual change, visible to everyone.

### Rating Snapshot Architecture

The sparkline cannot be accurately reconstructed from live survey data alone. Survey responses fall out of the active rating window after 12 months and are replaced when players update them — historical values are not preserved in the transactional database. The sparkline requires point-in-time snapshots stored deliberately.

**Monthly snapshots**

On the first of each month, a scheduled job calculates the current rating for every active subject — every candidate, brand, and product — and writes it to a `RatingSnapshot` table. The sparkline reads from this table. Accurate, fast, and the table grows predictably as the platform scales. At launch there are fewer than 24 data points but the window fills in over time.

**Database and replication**

HF uses **SQLite** as the primary application database, replicated continuously to **Google Cloud Storage** via **Litestream**. This provides live backup without managed database overhead — appropriate for the solo developer stage.

**BigQuery event streaming**

Every survey response creation or update is pushed to a **BigQuery** table from application code at the point of the event. No connector required — a straightforward write from the application layer when a survey event fires.

The BigQuery table is an immutable, append-only record of every survey event ever: who surveyed what, when, what they answered, and what the subject's rating was at that moment. Nothing is ever deleted from BigQuery even when responses fall out of the 12-month active window in SQLite.

BigQuery serves as:
- **Full audit trail** — every rating change and survey update permanently recorded
- **Historical reconstruction** — calculate what any subject's rating was at any point in history
- **Anomaly detection** — bulk submissions, coordinated rating attacks, and suspicious patterns visible in aggregate
- **Research data** — the strategic asset for demonstrating HF's influence to corporations and researchers
- **Snapshot fallback** — if a monthly snapshot is missed or corrupted, BigQuery has the raw data to reconstruct it

Storage cost is negligible at any realistic survey volume. Analytical queries run infrequently and cost proportionally.

Spendium rewards users for making purchases at more ethical businesses. The core insight is that consumer spending is one of the most frequent and impactful levers individuals have for signalling values and directing capital toward better actors.

### How the Loop Applies to Spendium

Spendium is the purchasing game. The three-stage loop operates as follows:

- **Survey → Rating** — players and non-players submit yes/no survey responses about stores, building a continuously updated ethics rating for each store
- **Rating → Interest** — players browse store ratings to find where to spend their money; higher-rated stores attract more ethical shoppers
- **Interest → Action** — players make purchases at higher-rated stores, earning points proportional to the store's rating; the purchase prompts a new survey, completing the circle

A player can enter at any point — by surveying a store they know, by browsing for a good store before shopping, or by making a purchase and being prompted to survey afterward.

### Points Model

The points a player earns from a purchase are calculated directly from the criteria that make up the subject's rating — not from a single aggregated rating number.

**Formula:**

> **points per dollar = Σ (criterion value × criterion probability)**

Where:
- **criterion value** — the weight assigned to that criterion, expressed directly in points
- **criterion probability** — the proportion of survey responses that answered "yes" to that criterion (e.g. 9 out of 10 surveys = 0.9)
- The sum is across all active criteria with at least k survey responses

> **total points = dollars spent × points per dollar**

**Example:**
- Criterion 1: value = 10 points, probability = 0.9 → contributes 9 points per dollar
- Criterion 2: value = 100 points, probability = 0.2 → contributes 20 points per dollar
- Points per dollar = 29. A $10 purchase earns 290 points.

There is no ceiling on points per dollar. As the ethical arms race matures and more criteria are satisfied at higher probabilities, points per dollar rises accordingly. The sky is the limit — this is the incentive mechanism that drives the arms race.

**What is shown on the profile**

The brand profile displays the current **points per dollar** figure prominently. Players see directly how many points they will earn per dollar spent there. A brand with 29 points per dollar versus one with 4 — the choice is obvious. This is the signal that drives ethical consumer behaviour.

**Membership multipliers**

Membership multipliers (1.5x for Members, 2x for Sustaining Members) are applied to the final points total after the criteria calculation. They scale the output, not the underlying criterion values.

**Minimum survey threshold (k)**

A criterion is only included in the points calculation if it has received at least **k = 5** survey responses. Criteria with fewer than k responses are excluded entirely — not treated as 0%. This prevents new criteria from contributing noise before meaningful community signal exists. k is configurable by HF administrators.

### Store Ethics Rating

Every brand in the system has a points-per-dollar figure derived from player-submitted surveys across all active criteria. The figure updates continuously as new survey responses come in. Responses older than 12 months fall out of the calculation automatically.

### Store Ethics Rating

Every store in the system has an ethics rating derived from player-submitted surveys. The rating is a simple average of all survey responses received within the past 12 months. Responses older than one year fall out of the rating automatically.

The simplicity of this model — unweighted average, open to anyone — is intentional. The reasoning behind this choice will be made explicit later in this document.

#### Survey Structure

Surveys consist of yes/no questions organised into categories. Each question maps to a specific criterion. A "yes" answer contributes positively to the store's rating; a "no" contributes negatively. Criteria are not equally weighted — some questions carry significantly more impact on the final rating than others. For example, a criterion about negative carbon footprint would outweigh a criterion about number of franchisees, because it more directly measures commons impact.

Anyone may submit a survey response for any store — players and non-players alike. This openness creates a real risk of inaccurate or manipulated ratings. This is a known and accepted design choice, for reasons that will be made explicit later.

#### Survey Responses and Conflict Resolution

When survey responses conflict (one respondent answers yes, another answers no to the same question), the simple average is used — there is no recency weighting and no verified-purchaser weighting. All responses are treated equally.

Each player has at most one active survey response per brand. Players may update their response after a cool-down period. See Survey Mechanics for the full re-survey, expiry, points, and visibility rules that apply across all games.

#### The Audit System

Crowd-sourced ratings are intentionally imperfect — they are noisy, open to manipulation, and directional rather than authoritative. This is by design. The community evidence layer — players, whistleblowers, and the public record — is the primary audit mechanism of HF. Corporate audits serve a specialist function on top of this community layer, covering only criteria that require domain expertise the crowd cannot provide.

A store or corporation may contact HF to request a formal audit. Audits are corporation-initiated. HF does not proactively audit brands. The audit result — a yes or no verdict on each audited criterion — is published publicly and locked for the duration of the audit window. The locked audit score applies to the audited criteria only; non-audited criteria remain community-rated. When the window expires, the score reverts to the crowd-sourced average until a new audit is requested.

The length of the effectiveness window varies by criterion, reflecting how quickly that dimension of ethical behaviour can change. Window lengths are determined by HF as part of the criteria governance process.

Audits are a paid service. Audit fees vary significantly by scope and complexity — auditing a global supply chain costs orders of magnitude more than auditing a small local brand. There is no standard price list. The fee for each audit is agreed between HF and the corporation and disclosed publicly before the audit begins.

**Corporations requesting audits must agree in advance that the result will be published regardless of outcome.** A corporation cannot suppress a negative audit result.

**Pre-Audit Public Disclosure**

Before any audit work begins, HF publishes the following for a mandatory **14-day community visibility window**:

1. **Corporation name** — who is being audited
2. **Criteria scope** — which specific criteria are covered, at which tier (2a publicly auditable or 2b privately auditable)
3. **Methodology** — how each criterion will be verified; what evidence is required; what constitutes a pass or fail
4. **Fee** — the agreed cost of this audit
5. **Timeline** — expected start and completion dates
6. **Auditor qualifications** — who will perform the audit and their relevant expertise and independence

The 14-day visibility window is not a community veto — audits do not require community approval. It is a transparency mechanism that allows members in good standing to raise constitutional concerns with the Constitutional Court before the work begins and the fee is paid. Publishing the methodology before the audit locks it — HF cannot quietly lower the bar mid-audit because the corporation is struggling to pass.

**Audit Transparency**

The fee is disclosed per audit, not as a standardised price list. Players can see the full scope and cost of every audit HF has performed. A corporation that paid for a comprehensive, expensive, multi-criteria audit and passed it has demonstrated something more meaningful than one that paid for a narrow audit of a single easily-satisfied criterion. The disclosure shows exactly what was and wasn't covered, preventing corporations from implying comprehensive ethical verification from a minimal audit.

**Audit Pricing Philosophy**

HF audit pricing reflects the expertise, independence, and consequential nature of the work. It is set at professional services rates appropriate to the complexity and scope of each audit — not at cost-recovery rates, and not at maximum extraction rates. HF does not underprice its work out of false modesty, nor does it extract maximum value from clients at the expense of mission integrity.

The goal is financial sustainability that enables HF to do its work well and indefinitely. An HF auditor's finding has real commercial impact on major corporations — a credible audit that improves a brand's rating influences real purchasing decisions at scale. That work is worth professional services rates. Undercharging would undermine HF's ability to attract qualified auditors, sustain operations, and invest in the mission.

Pricing is based on estimated hours at a professional services rate, agreed before the audit begins and disclosed publicly as part of the pre-audit disclosure package. As HF's player base grows and the demonstrated value of an HF rating becomes measurable, pricing may evolve — but the principle remains: fair value for consequential, expert, independent work. Not charity. Not extraction.

**Revenue Distribution**

Audit revenue will be redistributed to HF members and players in proportion to their accumulated points. The detailed design of this redistribution mechanism is an open task — see Open Questions Parking Lot. Redistribution does not begin until robust abuse controls are in place.


#### Criteria Weights

Criteria weights determine how much each yes/no question contributes to the overall brand rating. Weights are currently set by the founder. The long-term intention is for weights to be determined by HF members through the criteria governance process.

### Data Integration

The preferred approach is automatic capture of purchases via a financial data streaming service, so players do not need to manually log transactions. Plaid was the initial candidate but is primarily US-focused. The goal is an international solution. Alternative and complementary services should be evaluated.

Players must explicitly consent to linking their financial accounts. The integration must be designed with privacy and security as first-order requirements — not afterthoughts. Specific requirements to be designed include: data minimisation (capture only what is needed), encryption in transit and at rest, clear user controls for revoking access, and transparency about what is shared with HF and what is not.

### Manual Purchase Logging

Players should be able to log purchases manually as a fallback — covering cash purchases, unsupported banks, and international users not served by the primary integration. The design of the manual logging flow is an open task.

### Brand-Level Ratings

Surveys and ratings apply to the **brand or chain as a whole**, not to individual locations. HF is not a hygiene inspector — it is not asking "is this particular branch clean?" It is asking "does this brand operate ethically?" That question is almost always answered at the level of corporate policy, not individual franchise management.

Franchise owners who want to improve their location's ethical standing should advocate for change within their brand. The HF rating reflects the brand's choices, not the individual franchisee's.

This also significantly simplifies the database — one record per brand, not one per location.

### Open Design Questions

- **Manual logging flow** — UX and fraud considerations for self-reported purchases
- **Financial data integration** — identify the best international alternative(s) to Plaid
- **Privacy and security specification** — detailed requirements for financial data handling
- **Criteria governance** — how are new criteria proposed, evaluated, and adopted? (Cross-cutting concern — see Criteria Governance section)
- **Weights governance** — how does the member community ultimately determine criteria weights?
- **Rating manipulation** — the open survey model is vulnerable to coordinated abuse; mitigation strategies to be designed

## Players and Members

### Players

Anyone can play. Sign-up should be as simple as possible — the game must be accessible to the widest possible population. Players can take actions, earn points, submit surveys, and benefit from ratings without any fee or commitment. There is no barrier to participation.

### Members

HF has three membership levels. Governance rights are identical across all paid tiers — no tier confers more votes, more weight in criteria decisions, or more influence over constitutional matters than any other. This is a constitutional principle: governance cannot be purchased.

Membership is restricted to **human individuals only**. Corporations, organisations, and AI systems cannot become members. This is a constitutional principle, not a policy: Human Flourishing exists for human flourishing, and governance rights must remain with people.

#### Player — Free

- Earns and accumulates points
- Submits surveys and evidence
- Browses ratings and profiles
- No redistribution eligibility
- No governance rights

#### Member — $10/year

- Everything a Player gets
- **1.5x points multiplier** on all actions while membership is current
- Redistribution eligible — trailing 12-month points count toward annual distribution
- Full governance rights from Phase 2 onward — propose criteria, vote on proposals, refer adopted criteria to the Constitutional Court
- Member badge on profile and public survey responses
- **Founding Member badge** if joining during Phase 1 (before 100 members) — permanent, never available again, a historical marker of early commitment
- Access to members-only Discourse forum category with direct founder engagement

#### Sustaining Member — $50/year

- Everything a Member gets
- **2x points multiplier** on all actions while membership is current
- Sustaining Member badge — visibly distinct from standard Member badge
- Access to a Sustaining Member subcategory in the forum — a smaller, more intimate space for deeper governance discussion
- Same governance rights as Member — one vote, equal weight, no additional influence

#### Constitutional Notes

All point multipliers and fee amounts are configurable by HF administrators. The governance rights structure — flat across all paid tiers — is constitutional and cannot be changed without the full constitutional amendment process.

Court eligibility is open to any member in good standing regardless of tier. Financial commitment is not a proxy for constitutional wisdom or independence.

#### Revenue Distribution

A percentage of all HF revenue is distributed to members in proportion to their trailing 12-month points. Full redistribution design is in the Revenue Model and Redistribution section.

Non-members earn and accumulate points but receive no redistribution until they become members. Points accumulated before membership count within the trailing 12-month window — historical engagement is rewarded when a player joins.

## Spendium MVP & Roadmap

### MVP Philosophy

The core value proposition of Spendium is: **ethical spending feels rewarding and influences others**. The MVP must prove that the core loop works — survey, rating, interest, action — with the minimum infrastructure required to make it trustworthy enough to be worth playing.

### What the MVP Must Have

1. **Account creation** — as frictionless as possible. Email or social login. No membership required to start.
2. **Manual purchase logging** — no financial data integration at this stage. Users log purchases by hand. Slower, but gives real signal about whether people want to play before investing in complex infrastructure.
3. **A brand database with basic search** — users need to find the brand they bought from. Start with a small curated list. Let users suggest missing brands.
4. **A simple survey** — one category, a handful of yes/no questions. Enough to produce a meaningful rating. Full criteria framework comes later.
5. **Points display** — users must see their points immediately after logging a purchase. The reward feedback must be instant and visible.
6. **A basic brand rating page** — so users can see the impact of their surveys. This closes the loop.

### What Can Wait

- Plaid or any automated transaction capture
- Membership, governance, and criteria weighting
- Audits and the revenue model
- Revenue distribution to players and members
- Polium and Humanium
- Multiple survey categories

### Roadmap

#### Stage 1 — Prove the Loop
Manual logging, basic brand database, one survey category, points, brand rating page. Ship to a small closed group of 50–100 trusted early users. Goal: does the feedback loop feel rewarding? Do people come back?

> **Breakpoint 1: Survey quality vs. quantity tension.** Real users will immediately reveal that yes/no criteria produce inconsistent results — not because the system is broken, but because the questions are ambiguous. Initial criteria will almost certainly need to be rewritten based on what confuses people. Do not cement the criteria framework until after this stage.

#### Stage 2 — Grow the Brand Database
Open sign-up. Let players suggest and add brands. Build a lightweight moderation process. Goal: does the community want to expand the system, or does engagement drop off?

> **Breakpoint 2: The cold start problem.** Most brands will have zero or one survey response. A rating based on one opinion is nearly meaningless. A decision is needed: show ratings with low-confidence warnings, hide them until a threshold is reached, or show everything and accept the noise. Player behaviour will determine which approach retains trust.

#### Stage 3 — Introduce Weighted Criteria and Multiple Categories
By this stage real survey data exists. Evidence-based decisions can be made about which criteria people find meaningful, which are ambiguous, and which need reweighting. This is when the criteria governance process begins to matter in practice.

> **Breakpoint 3: The gaming problem becomes real.** As soon as Spendium has enough users to matter, stores will notice. Some will encourage customers to submit positive surveys. Coordinated rating inflation will appear. This is good news — it means the system has influence — but a response must be ready. This is the natural trigger point for introducing audits as the credible, manipulation-proof alternative.

#### Stage 4 — Launch the Audit Product
First paying corporate customers. This validates the business model and funds further development. The audit process must be fully designed before this stage begins, not during it.

> **Breakpoint 4: Revenue distribution becomes urgent.** Once money is flowing in, members and players will expect the redistribution promise to be honoured. Abuse controls must be in place before this stage. Do not reach Stage 4 without a credible redistribution design.

#### Stage 5 — Automate Transaction Capture and Launch Product Ratings
Only at this stage does financial data integration (Plaid or equivalent) make sense. By now the system has proven people want to play, the data requirements are well understood, and the trust and compliance infrastructure exists to handle financial data responsibly.

POS integration is the gateway to a fundamental expansion of Spendium's scope. Where the current game rates **stores**, POS data reveals **what was bought** — opening up the rating and auditing of individual **products**. This is the ultimate goal for Spendium.

**Product ratings** work alongside store ratings. The points formula becomes additive:

> **Points = (dollars × store rating) + (dollars × product rating)**

A player who is thoughtful about both where they shop and what they buy earns significantly more than one who optimises only one dimension. The game deepens naturally as players engage more — stores first, then products — without ever feeling overwhelming at the start.

**Implications of product ratings:**
- Manufacturers and brands become a new audit customer base, entirely separate from retailers
- The audit surface area expands dramatically: ingredients, supply chain, packaging, labour practices, carbon footprint per unit, and more
- A store with a mediocre ethics rating may carry exemplary products — and vice versa
- Stores are incentivised to curate their shelves ethically, not just their policies, because stocking higher-rated products drives more player foot traffic
- The HF database grows from a list of stores to a catalogue of products — a substantially larger and more valuable infrastructure

The interaction between store and product ratings is not yet fully designed. The additive formula above is the current working assumption, to be validated by evidence once both rating types exist in the system.

> **Breakpoint 5: International expansion reality check.** Automated transaction capture will work smoothly in some markets and not at all in others. This is where the international ambition will require either parallel infrastructure or a phased geographic rollout.

> **Breakpoint 6: Product database scale.** A brand database is manageable. A product catalogue is orders of magnitude larger. The moderation, verification, and governance challenges of maintaining product-level data at scale will require dedicated design before this stage is complete.

### Critical Risk
Stage 1 is where the idea lives or dies. If the feedback loop does not feel intrinsically rewarding to a small group of motivated early users, no amount of financial engineering or governance design will save it. Almost all early energy should go into making that loop feel genuinely good to use.

## Spendium Stage 1 Experience Design

### Core Constraints
- Spendium is a game about physical commerce. Online purchases are excluded by Article 2 of the HF constitution — profound human connection requires physical presence and human exchange. Spendium is explicitly a game about local commerce.
- The experience must work whether or not a store is participating in HF, and whether or not a store is in the HF database.
- Evidence of purchase must be collected without creating prohibitive friction.

### Evidence of Purchase

Four mechanisms, in descending order of trust and point value:

1. **QR code scan** — store displays a static QR code at the till. Customer scans at moment of payment. Store ID is verified automatically. Purchase amount is self-reported by the customer. Full points.
2. **Receipt photo** — customer photographs their receipt. Verifiable but requires processing. Used for non-participating physical stores. Full points.
3. **Trust-based self-report** — customer logs a physical purchase manually with no verification. Earns points at **0.5x**. An explicit fallback, not the default.
4. **Online purchase** — verifiable via order confirmation but excludes the physical human connection that Article 2 requires. Earns points at **0.5x**, consistent with the trust-based tier.

The reduced value for online purchases is not a penalty — it is an accurate reflection of the game's values. Physical commerce creates human connection; online commerce does not. The points system is a behaviour-shaping tool, and the hierarchy should make the preferred behaviour clear.

### Store QR Codes

Participating stores receive a **single static QR code** that encodes their store ID. The code does not change per transaction and requires no point-of-sale integration. A store joins HF and receives a printable QR code immediately — participation requires nothing more than printing one piece of paper and displaying it at the till.

The customer scans the code, the PWA opens pre-populated with the store name, and the customer self-reports the purchase amount. Receipt photo provides optional verification for full points.

**POS system integration** — building plugins for Square, Shopify, Toast, Clover and other major systems would allow transaction amounts to be verified automatically and QR codes to be generated per transaction. This is the right long-term goal but is a Stage 3 or 4 problem. The complexity and fragmentation of POS systems makes this inappropriate for Stage 1.

### The Four Scenarios

**Scenario A — Participating store, in HF database** *(full experience)*
The store displays its HF QR code at the till. The customer scans it at the moment of payment. The PWA opens instantly — no app install required — pre-populated with the store name. The customer confirms the purchase amount and earns full points immediately. A short survey of three to five yes/no questions appears. If completed in-store, a survey bonus is awarded. If dismissed, the survey is delivered by email within the hour. The store's rating updates visibly.

**Scenario B — Non-participating brand, in HF database**
No QR code is available. The customer opens the app, searches for the brand, logs the purchase amount, and photographs their receipt. Slightly more friction, same point value for the purchase, no in-store survey bonus. Survey is delivered by email.

**Scenario C — Non-participating brand, not in HF database**
Customer opens the app, types the brand name, logs it as a new brand suggestion, and photographs their receipt. Earns trust-based points at 0.5x until the receipt is verified and the brand is added to the database. Survey is delivered by email once the brand is added. This scenario is valuable — it is the primary mechanism by which the brand database grows.

**Scenario D — Participating store, not in HF database**
Unlikely if participation requires registration with HF. Flag as an edge case to handle later.

### The Email Survey

When a survey is not completed in-store, an email is sent within the hour. Requirements:
- Three questions maximum at Stage 1 — answerable in under a minute
- Embedded directly in the email body where possible — no app open required
- Followed by a points update notification on submission

### Benefits of Store Participation

A store that registers with HF receives:
- A static QR code for immediate deployment
- Visibility and searchability in the HF app
- Eligibility to request a paid audit — only participating stores can do so
- An HF-aware badge in the app

Stores are recruited organically by players asking why their favourite store is not on HF. No cold outreach required from HF at Stage 1.

## Polium

Polium rewards ethical voting. Players survey political candidates against HF criteria, building a community-generated picture of each candidate's alignment with human flourishing. That picture drives voting interest and ultimately voting behaviour.

### How the Loop Applies to Polium

Polium is the voting game. The three-stage loop operates as follows:

- **Survey → Rating** — players and non-players submit yes/no survey responses about candidates throughout their careers, building a continuously updated ethics rating for each candidate
- **Rating → Interest** — players browse candidate ratings in the run-up to an election; higher-rated candidates attract more declared support and public commitment
- **Interest → Action** — players declare they will vote for — or have voted for — the highest HF-rated candidate, earning full points; the declaration may be shared publicly on social media for an additional multiplier

A player can enter at any point — by surveying a candidate they know something about, by browsing ratings before deciding how to vote, or by declaring a vote and being prompted to survey the candidate afterward.

### What Polium Is Not

Polium is not a get-out-the-vote initiative. Voting for its own sake is a neutral act — voting against the goals of human flourishing is actively detrimental and should not be rewarded. Polium is explicitly a human flourishing initiative, and makes no apology for caring about *how* people vote, not merely *whether* they vote.

This is consistent with Spendium's logic: players do not earn points for spending money — they earn points for spending money at an ethical store. The ethical dimension is what is rewarded, not the action alone.

### Candidate Endorsement

Candidates may endorse the HF rating system by placing a unique HF-generated link on their official campaign website. The link is issued by HF and is unique to each candidate. No manual review is required — endorsement status is determined entirely by whether the link is present when crawled.

**Verification:** HF crawls each candidate's registered URL at a random time every 24–48 hours. If the link is found, the candidate is endorsed. If it is not found, they are not. There is no history, no penalty, and no cooling-off period — status simply reflects the current state of the link.

**Eligibility:** Any candidate may endorse, regardless of their HF rating. Note that endorsement is the first step in the accountability chain — a candidate cannot be blacklisted without having first endorsed. Endorsing is therefore a meaningful public commitment, not a trivial action.

**Effect on players:** A player who declares a vote for an endorsed candidate has a **2.0 modifier** applied to the criteria-based declaration points. If the candidate's base declaration points are 183, an endorsing player earns 366 points. The endorsement has no effect on the candidate's criteria or survey responses — it only affects the modifier applied at declaration.

**The arms race dynamic:** Once one candidate in a race endorses HF, their opponent faces an implicit choice: endorse and compete on ratings, or decline and be visibly absent from the system. Absence reads as an implicit acknowledgement that the candidate does not believe their record will bear scrutiny. This pressure operates structurally — HF does not need to say anything for it to work.

**What endorsement is not:** It is not a mechanism for candidates to influence their rating. The community's assessment remains entirely community-generated. The candidate is endorsing the process, not the outcome.

### Points Model

- **Survey submission** — **100 points** per first survey submitted, configurable. 50 points on second survey of the same candidate, 25 points permanently thereafter. Awarded immediately on submission. See Survey Mechanics for the full re-survey, cool-down, expiry, and visibility rules.
- **Declared vote alignment** — points = Σ (criterion value × criterion probability) × modifier (1.0 standard, 2.0 endorsed, 0.25 blacklisted), self-reported, trust-based
- **Social media share** — flat 50-point bonus for sharing a vote declaration via the in-app share button. See Social Media Share Mechanics for full details.

Players declare that they voted for — or intend to vote for — the highest HF-rated candidate. This declaration is unverified and earns full points regardless. This is a deliberate philosophical and behavioural design choice, not a loophole.

**Why full points for an unverified declaration?** Behavioural science is clear that public commitment to a stated intention significantly increases the likelihood of following through. The declaration has genuine causal power — it nudges the player toward actually voting in alignment, and it signals to candidates that a growing community of engaged citizens is tracking their HF score and acting on it. The ethical arms race dynamic from Spendium emerges here too: candidates competing to be the most HF-aligned because it demonstrably influences votes.

**Social media sharing** extends the commitment effect further. A public declaration on social media is a stronger behavioural nudge than a private one within the app, and turns every player into an organic distribution channel for HF. The share is initiated from within the app via a single button — the post is pre-populated with the candidate's HF profile link and a card image, requiring no effort from the player. Follower-count weighting is explicitly excluded — it introduces complexity and creates perverse incentives toward vanity metrics. See Social Media Share Mechanics for full details.

This is a publicly defensible position rooted in the mission. HF is not neutral about how people vote. A candidate who scores poorly on HF criteria is, by definition, not oriented toward making the world a wonderful place for everyone forever. That is a values statement consistent with Article 1 of the constitution.

### Candidate Accountability

The survey process is continuous — not just in the run-up to an election but throughout a candidate's entire career in public life. Pre-election and post-election surveys measure different things:

- **Pre-election surveys** — community opinion on how likely the candidate is to uphold HF criteria. Based on stated positions, track record, and public record.
- **Post-election surveys** — community assessment of how well the candidate is actually upholding HF criteria in office. Based on voting record, policy decisions, public statements, and behaviour in power.

This distinction is important but the mechanism is the same. The community maintains a persistent, continuously updated record of each candidate's alignment with human flourishing values. Promises made before an election are measured against actions taken after it. Betrayal is visible, surveyable, and consequential.

#### Political Audits

Any candidate may request an HF audit of their public record. A candidate who believes their voting record and behaviour in office reflects strong HF alignment has a clear incentive to seek an audit — it replaces noisy crowd survey data with a locked, credible, evidence-based score. This mirrors the store audit in Spendium exactly: the entity seeking credibility initiates the process and bears the cost of transparency and scrutiny.

The raw material for a political audit is entirely public: voting records, parliamentary transcripts, policy positions, financial disclosures, and public statements. This makes political audits potentially stronger than store audits — the evidence base is objective, public, and cannot be withheld.

Audits are also available to any candidate who wants to demonstrate their record is strong. Since blacklisting is now permanent with no exit path, audits no longer serve as a route out of blacklisting — they serve as a credibility-building tool for candidates who have not yet been blacklisted.

#### Blacklisting

Blacklisting is reserved for politicians who made an explicit public commitment to HF values, benefited from that commitment at the ballot box, and then governed against those values. A low rating alone does not trigger blacklisting — the rating already communicates community assessment. Blacklisting is a specific statement about betrayal of trust.

**Trigger conditions — all three must be met:**

1. The candidate endorsed HF before an election — they placed the HF link on their official campaign website and benefited from the 2x points multiplier
2. An HF administrator has confirmed, following community deliberation in the forum, that the candidate won their election
3. The candidate's post-election rating has fallen below **half of their pre-election high** and has remained there for **90 consecutive days**

The threshold scales with the commitment the candidate demonstrated before the election. A candidate who endorsed with strong community support is held to a proportionally higher standard — because they set that standard themselves.

Examples:
- Pre-election high of 80% → blacklist threshold is below 40%
- Pre-election high of 60% → blacklist threshold is below 30%
- Pre-election high of 30% → blacklist threshold is below 15%

A candidate who endorsed with modest community support has to govern truly terribly to be blacklisted. A candidate who endorsed with strong community support is held proportionally higher because they set that standard themselves.

All thresholds are configurable by HF administrators without a code deployment, including the ratio (currently 50%) and the sustained period (currently 90 days).

**Blacklisting is permanent.** There is no exit path. Unlike a low rating — which reflects current community assessment and can improve — blacklisting reflects a documented betrayal of a public commitment. The record stands regardless of future behaviour.

**Effects of blacklisting:**

- **Permanent endorsement bar** — a blacklisted politician can never re-endorse HF. The 2x endorsement multiplier is permanently unavailable to them.
- **Points multiplier on declarations** — declaring a vote for a blacklisted candidate applies a **0.25 modifier** to the criteria-based declaration points. If the candidate's base declaration points are 183, a declaring player earns 45.75 points. The modifier does not affect the underlying criteria calculation — it scales the final output. HF always rewards engagement; the reduced modifier signals trust betrayal without silencing the community's assessment.
- **Prominent blacklist notice** on their profile with date and reason — not just a low rating but an explicit statement of betrayal
- **Permanent historical record** — never removed, even if the politician improves their rating or changes office

**Blacklisting follows the individual**, not the office. A blacklisted politician carries that status into any future election for any position.

**Process:** Blacklisting is not automatic. It requires an HF administrator to confirm the election win and authorise the process, following community deliberation in the forum. The admin action is publicly documented with a link to the forum discussion. This is a serious and permanent statement — it warrants one human checkpoint.

#### Points Model Consistency

The same criteria-based formula applies to both Spendium and Polium. The underlying mechanism is identical — the action type determines what the base unit is.

**Spendium (purchases):**
> **total points = dollars spent × Σ (criterion value × criterion probability)**

**Polium (vote declarations):**
> **total points = Σ (criterion value × criterion probability) × modifier**

Where modifier is:
- **1.0** — standard candidate
- **2.0** — endorsed candidate
- **0.25** — blacklisted candidate

In both games, criteria values are expressed directly in points, criterion probability is the proportion of survey responses that answered "yes", and only criteria with at least k = 5 responses are included. There is no ceiling. As the ethical arms race matures and more criteria are satisfied at higher probabilities, point totals rise accordingly.

**What is shown on the profile**

The candidate profile displays the current **base declaration points** — the raw sum before any modifier is applied. A candidate showing 4,533 points versus one showing 34 points — the choice is obvious. Modifiers are applied at the point of declaration, not displayed on the profile.

**Membership multipliers**

Applied to the final points total after all other calculations. They scale the output, not the underlying criterion values.



### Candidate Profile Enrichment

Beyond the minimum registration fields (name, jurisdiction, office, election, external reference URL), the community can enrich a candidate profile with:

- **Additional external reference URLs** — Wikipedia, campaign site, LinkedIn, news profiles
- **Biography** — short free-text description of the candidate's background and positions
- **Photo URL** — a link to a publicly available photo (no uploads)
- **Social media links** — Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok handles
- **Party or political affiliation**
- **Office history** — already a protected field, community-maintained

Free-form comments are explicitly excluded — evidence is the mechanism for that.

### Candidate Visibility

The top **10** candidates by engagement are surfaced by default for any given election. Engagement is defined as the number of unique players who have surveyed or otherwise interacted with a candidate profile. Everything else is searchable but not prominently shown. Players who want to find a candidate outside the top 10 can search by name.

### What Is Being Rated

Candidates only. Parties and policies are derivatives of candidate behaviour — a party is a collection of candidates, and a policy is something a candidate voted for or against. Both are fully captured by rating the candidate comprehensively, including their voting record, stated positions, and whether their actions in office matched their declared HF alignment. Rating parties and policies as independent entities would add complexity without adding signal.

### Democratic Integrity

The secret ballot is a sacred cultural and legal norm. Polium does not attempt to verify how anyone voted and has no interest in doing so. The self-declaration is voluntary and private within the app. No mechanism exists — or should ever exist — within HF that records or audits actual votes cast.

Verifying that someone voted — without knowing how — is already socially acceptable in many jurisdictions via voter rolls and proof-of-voting receipts. Polium does not use even this mechanism, since the points model rewards declared alignment rather than the act of voting.

### Resolved Design Decisions

- **Candidate participation** — deferred until the game matures. No decision needed at launch.
- **Negative scores** — no negative scores. The floor is zero. Players always have an incentive to engage regardless of how poorly a candidate rates.
- **First-time candidates** — treated identically to any other candidate. No special caveats or weighting. Thin signal is thin signal.

The ultimate goal of Spendium — and of the game suite as a whole — is not to reward ethical behaviour. It is to **create the conditions for an ethical arms race** amongst manufacturers, brands, and retailers, forcing them to compete with each other and with themselves on ethical grounds because their customers are materially rewarded for choosing the more ethical option.

The game works without POS integration, and that is a deliberate strength. Every stage of the roadmap delivers independent value. POS integration is an accelerant that deepens the game dramatically — a store with POS integration becomes vastly more attractive to players, because every product purchase earns additional points — but the core loop is sound without it.

### Greenwashing and Ethical Theatre

The history of corporate ethics programmes is largely a history of ethical theatre: certifications that sound meaningful, criteria designed to be easy to satisfy, audits that rubber-stamp predetermined outcomes. Spendium must actively resist becoming another vehicle for this.

The structural defences built into the system are:

- **Member-governed criteria** — the people who set the standards have no financial interest in making them easy to meet
- **Public criteria and weights** — nothing is hidden; every criterion and its weight is visible and challengeable by anyone
- **The Constitutional Court** — a backstop against criteria being quietly captured by corporate influence over time
- **Survey noise as a continuous check** — even a locked audit score sits alongside live public perception; a company cannot fully escape its crowd rating
- **Time-bounded audits** — no score is permanent; corporations must continually re-earn their rating

### Regulatory Capture: The Known Risk

As audit revenue grows, HF will face a structural financial incentive to keep corporate customers happy. This is the classic regulatory capture problem: the regulator becomes dependent on the regulated, and standards soften imperceptibly over time.

This risk must be named, not managed away. The governance structure — member-controlled criteria, public weights, an independent Constitutional Court — is designed specifically to resist it. But the design must be stress-tested against this failure mode at every stage. Any process that allows corporations to influence criteria, weights, or audit methodology, even indirectly, must be treated as a constitutional threat.

*This section applies across all HF games — Spendium, Polium, and Humanium.*

Criteria are the yes/no questions that form the basis of all surveys across all games. They are organised into categories. The process for proposing, evaluating, and adopting criteria is a core part of how HF operationalises Article 3 of its constitution (the weight of evidence wins).

### Current State

At launch, criteria are determined by the founder. This is a temporary arrangement. The long-term goal is a fully public and governed process.

### Principles

- The process for submitting criteria must be publicly available and transparent
- Criteria must be answerable with a simple yes or no
- Criteria should be clearly connected to commons impact — they must be justifiable under the HF constitution
- The weight assigned to each criterion must also be publicly visible and justified

### Open Design Questions

- What is the full lifecycle of a criterion — proposal, review, trial, adoption, retirement?
- Who evaluates proposed criteria, and by what standard?
- How are criteria weights set by the membership, and what does that voting or deliberation process look like?
- How does the Constitutional Court adjudicate disputes about whether a criterion is constitutional?

## Humanium

Humanium rewards ethical life choices. It is the third game in the HF suite and shares the same core gameplay loop — surveys shape ratings, ratings drive interest, interest drives action. The subject being assessed is the player's own life rather than an external entity, which makes it the most personal and philosophically rich of the three games.

Humanium is a stretch goal. Phase 1 launches only when Spendium and Polium are well established. Phase 2 and the accessibility feature build on a mature player base.

### Core Mechanic

The trigger is a scheduled check-in — periodic, like a scheduled service interval. At their chosen cadence (configurable, suggested default 90 days), a player is prompted to reflect on their life choices across categories they have opted into. They answer yes/no questions mapped to criteria. Points are earned immediately based on the criteria-based formula:

> **check-in points = Σ (criterion value × criterion probability × evidence multiplier)**

The check-in is primarily a self-reflection tool. Most of its value is private. Public signalling is available but always optional.

### Categories and Criteria

Criteria in Humanium describe personal life choices. Example categories:

**Transport** — car-free, small car, electric vehicle, public transit primary user, cycling primary user
**Diet** — plant-based, low meat consumption, locally sourced, low food waste
**Energy** — renewable home energy, low consumption
**Community** — regular volunteering, civic participation, local commerce preference
**Work** — ethical employer, fair labour practices accepted
**Consumption** — repair over replace, secondhand purchasing, low waste

Criteria must be applicable across different life circumstances, income levels, and geographies. Criteria that are only achievable by wealthy or urban players are constitutionally suspect under Article 1. The criteria governance process must be especially attentive to this in Humanium.

### Evidence Multipliers

Players can submit evidence to HF privately — bank statements, transit records, utility bills, app data — to earn a points multiplier on their self-declared check-in answers. Evidence is verified by HF but never published. The player earns the multiplier without exposing the underlying document.

Evidence tiers:
- **Self-declared:** 1x — base criterion value, no evidence required
- **Community-corroborated:** 1.5x — others who know the player have confirmed the claim through the community evidence mechanic
- **Privately verified by HF:** 3x — verifiable artefacts submitted to and confirmed by HF

The social broadcast is a separate voluntary act. A player can share their check-in results publicly via the social share mechanic for additional points. The broadcast is the declaration; the evidence stays private.

### Phase 1 — Solo Check-ins

The foundational mechanic. Scheduled prompts, yes/no criteria questions, private self-assessment, optional private evidence submission, optional public social broadcast. No other players involved. Pure reflection.

### Phase 2 — Meeting Groups

Players host or attend regular in-person gatherings at semi-public venues — community centres, places of worship, library meeting rooms, workplace community spaces. The format is modelled on values-based community gatherings that have existed across cultures and traditions for centuries: structured reflection, shared commitment to living well, non-judgment, witness, accountability. The meeting group is Humanium's most direct expression of Article 2 — profound human connections, in person, between people who might never otherwise have met.

**The HF Meeting Format**

HF provides a defined meeting format that hosts commit to following when they register a group. The format is not a rigid script — it defines the structure and the spirit of the gathering while leaving room for authentic human interaction. It covers: how the meeting opens, how check-ins are shared, how the group responds to disclosures, and how the meeting closes. The format is designed to be culturally adaptable — it works across different traditions, backgrounds, and worldviews.

The format evolves through the community governance process, but at any given time there is a canonical version that hosts are expected to follow.

Whether the meeting followed the HF format is one of the primary survey criteria evaluated by attendees after each meeting. A host who consistently runs meetings that diverge significantly from the format will see that reflected in the group's survey rating.

**How it works:**

A player registers a meeting group with HF — venue, host, cadence, approximate size. HF issues a QR code for the group. Attendees scan in at the start of each meeting. HF records attendance and awards points.

**Verification via surveys:**

The meeting group itself becomes a subject that gets surveyed after each meeting. Primary survey criteria:
- Was the HF meeting format followed?
- Was this a genuine reflection space?
- Did the host facilitate well?
- Was it safe and welcoming?

The crowd verifies the crowd. A meeting group with consistently good survey responses is legitimate and well-functioning. A group with poor responses depreciates naturally through the Community Lifecycle Management mechanism.

**Points for meeting groups:**
- **Attending** — points per attendance, scaled by the group's survey rating
- **Hosting** — higher points than attending, reflecting the additional commitment
- **Hosting a consistently high-rated group** — verified host badge

**Growing a meeting group**

Meeting groups find their own momentum when the conditions are right. A host who registers a group, shares it through local channels, and runs a good first meeting will attract attendees organically. HF supports discovery — registered groups are visible to players in the same jurisdiction — but does not orchestrate growth. The community does that.

### Accessibility Feature — Peer Accountability Calls

For players who cannot attend in-person meeting groups — geographic isolation, disability, schedule constraints — a peer accountability call provides an equivalent experience. This runs alongside meeting groups permanently as an accessibility feature, not as a phase that gets superseded.

A player opts in as a caller, a recipient, or both. Callers are identity-verified by HF before participating. Recipients explicitly opt in knowing a stranger will call. Calls follow a strict structured format consistent with the spirit of the HF meeting format. All calls are recorded with both parties' explicit consent, disclosed at opt-in. Recipients can flag inappropriate behaviour with serious consequences for the caller. The caller does not know who they are calling and will likely never call the same person twice.

Legal compliance around call recording varies by jurisdiction — specialist legal review required before launch.

**Points for accountability calls:**
- **Caller** — points for completing a structured call
- **Recipient** — points equivalent to a solo check-in
- **Both** — social share option following the call

### What Humanium Is Not

Humanium is not a surveillance platform. The tattletale mechanic — where players report on each other's private behaviour — was explicitly considered and rejected. It would poison human relationships, invert the positive incentive philosophy, and create infrastructure for targeted harassment.

Humanium is not a virtue signalling machine. Self-reflection is primary. Public broadcast is secondary and always optional. A player who makes profound changes in their private life and never broadcasts a single one is playing Humanium exactly as intended.

### Open Design Questions

- **The HF meeting format** — the canonical meeting structure needs to be designed before Phase 2 launches; it should be simple, culturally adaptable, and rooted in the constitutional articles
- **Criteria design** — must be binary, culturally universal, applicable across income levels and geographies; the criteria governance process must be especially attentive to this
- **Evidence verification at scale** — private evidence review requires a defined process at scale, possibly involving trusted community reviewers
- **Meeting group safety** — hosts are the primary safety mechanism; HF needs a clear process for handling reports of unsafe group environments
- **Accountability call legal framework** — call recording consent requirements vary by jurisdiction; specialist legal review required before launch
- **Check-in cadence design** — whether different criteria categories warrant different cadences or a single configurable cadence is sufficient

## The Ethical Arms Race

The ultimate goal of Spendium — and of the game suite as a whole — is not to reward ethical behaviour. It is to **create the conditions for an ethical arms race** amongst manufacturers, brands, retailers, and politicians, forcing them to compete with each other and with themselves on ethical grounds because their customers and voters are materially rewarded for choosing the more ethical option.

### Greenwashing and Ethical Theatre

The history of corporate ethics programmes is largely a history of ethical theatre: certifications that sound meaningful, criteria designed to be easy to satisfy, audits that rubber-stamp predetermined outcomes. Spendium must actively resist becoming another vehicle for this.

The structural defences built into the system are:

- **Member-governed criteria** — the people who set the standards have no financial interest in making them easy to meet
- **Public criteria and weights** — nothing is hidden; every criterion and its weight is visible and challengeable by anyone
- **The Constitutional Court** — a backstop against criteria being quietly captured by corporate influence over time
- **Survey noise as a continuous check** — even a locked audit score sits alongside live public perception; a company cannot fully escape its crowd rating
- **Time-bounded audits** — no score is permanent; corporations must continually re-earn their rating

### Regulatory Capture: The Known Risk

As audit revenue grows, HF will face a structural financial incentive to keep corporate customers happy. This is the classic regulatory capture problem: the regulator becomes dependent on the regulated, and standards soften imperceptibly over time.

This risk must be named, not managed away. The governance structure — member-controlled criteria, public weights, an independent Constitutional Court — is designed specifically to resist it. But the design must be stress-tested against this failure mode at every stage. Any process that allows corporations to influence criteria, weights, or audit methodology, even indirectly, must be treated as a constitutional threat.

## Legal Structure

HF incorporates as a **Delaware C-Corporation**. This is a deliberately simple starting point chosen for speed, cost, and flexibility — not as the permanent destination.

### The Conversion Commitment

HF is committed to converting to a **member-owned cooperative** once the organisation demonstrates genuine, sustained financial viability. This commitment is public, documented here, and written into the founding corporate records. It is not a vague intention — it has defined trigger conditions that no individual can override.

**Conversion is triggered when all three of the following conditions are met:**

1. Monthly operating revenue has exceeded monthly operating costs for **12 consecutive months**, excluding any revenue source that represents more than **40% of total revenue** in that period — preventing a single large audit contract from artificially satisfying this condition
2. Membership dues alone cover baseline monthly operating costs — testing whether HF survives without any new audit contracts
3. HF holds a minimum **6-month operating reserve** in liquid funds at the point of conversion

All three conditions must be met simultaneously. When they are, the founder initiates conversion at their discretion — but **no later than 5 years from the date of incorporation** regardless of whether the conditions have been met. The hard deadline is the trust mechanism: members are not relying on a promise of eventual conversion; they are relying on a date.

Conversion is a known legal process. A specialist in US cooperative law is engaged at the point of incorporation to document the intended conversion path, so it is not designed under time pressure when the trigger is hit.

### Pre-Conversion: Membership Fees as Operating Revenue

Before conversion, membership fees are **operating revenue** — they fund the organisation's work. They are not an investment and carry no promise of financial return. Members who join before conversion are funding the build, not investing in the redistribution model. This is stated clearly in the membership agreement before any fee is paid.

Points accumulated before conversion are honoured in the first post-conversion distribution. Members who built up points during the pre-conversion period benefit from their early engagement — their participation record is preserved and counts in the first cooperative distribution. Early members are not penalised for joining before the redistribution model was live; they are rewarded for it.

### Post-Conversion: Financial Transparency and Independent Audit

Once HF converts to a cooperative structure, the following financial governance applies:

**Member-level financial transparency** — HF publishes quarterly financial reports to all members. Reports are structured as follows:

*Revenue*
- Total audit fees received (aggregate — individual client fees are not disclosed)
- Total membership dues received
- Other income

*Costs*
- People costs (aggregate — individual salaries are not disclosed)
- Infrastructure and technology
- Legal and compliance
- Audit delivery costs
- Administrative and overhead

*Position*
- Operating surplus or deficit for the period
- Reserve balance
- Total system points in circulation (the denominator every member needs to verify their own redistribution share)
- A short plain-English narrative from the founder or board explaining anything material

The quarterly report is published to members before any distribution is made. Members who want to verify their redistribution share divide their trailing 12-month points by the total system points figure in the report.

**Independent third-party audit** — annually, HF engages an independent auditor to verify that the underlying figures — salaries, costs, revenue — are accurately represented in the member-facing summary. The auditor's opinion is published alongside the annual summary. Members see the auditor's conclusion; the underlying detail remains confidential. The auditor is selected by member vote from a shortlist of two or three qualified firms proposed by the board. This makes the auditor accountable to the membership rather than solely to the founders.

Individual salaries, individual audit client fees, and vendor-level spending are not disclosed publicly or to members. These exclusions protect HF's negotiating position, its ability to attract and retain staff, and its audit clients' reasonable confidentiality expectations — without compromising the member community's ability to verify that the organisation is being run honestly.

### Member Profile: Points and Redistribution Share

Each member's profile displays:

- **Total points earned** — their cumulative participation score
- **Points by activity type** — breakdown by surveys submitted, purchases logged, votes declared, etc.
- **Redistribution share** — their trailing 12-month points as a percentage of total system points (sourced from the most recent quarterly report)
- **Status** — pre-conversion (accruing, not yet payable) or post-conversion (payable per the annual distribution schedule)
- **Distribution history** — once redistribution begins, a record of amounts received and dates

No dollar figures appear on member profiles until post-conversion redistribution is live. Points are a participation record, not a dollar claim. Their cash value is unknown until the cooperative structure is in place, the distribution pool is known, and the board sets the rate for each period. Showing speculative dollar figures before that moment would create false expectations and potential legal exposure.

## Revenue Model and Redistribution

### Revenue Sources

HF has two primary revenue sources:

- **Membership fees** — $10/year per member. Small and predictable. Scales with community size. Expected to cover a portion of operating expenses.
- **Audit fees** — the primary long-term revenue source. Variable, scales with corporate engagement and HF's demonstrated influence on purchasing behaviour. Priced at professional services rates appropriate to the scope and complexity of each audit.

### Cost Buckets

- **Operating expenses** — infrastructure, legal, admin
- **Audit performance costs** — the direct cost of performing each audit, including contracted specialist auditors
- **Salaries** — founder and future staff
- **Marketing** — two distinct activities: player acquisition (bringing people into the games) and corporate outreach (approaching corporations with data and proposals to pursue an audit)
- **Redistribution** — returned to members in proportion to engagement

### Redistribution Commitments

HF has two redistribution commitments that run simultaneously. Members receive whichever produces the higher payout in any given distribution period.

**Commitment 1 — Revenue floor**

HF redistributes a minimum percentage of all gross revenue regardless of costs or profit:

- **Under $1,000,000 gross revenue:** 5% to redistribution
- **$1,000,000 and above:** 10% to redistribution

This floor is a constitutional commitment, not an accounting line item. It cannot be argued away by cost classification. The threshold is based on gross revenue, not profit — it applies even in loss-making years.

**Commitment 2 — Profit share**

Once HF covers all costs, **50% of remaining profit** is redistributed to members. This commitment is fixed from day one. It only triggers when profit exists, so it cannot bankrupt HF in lean years.

### Who Receives Redistribution

Members only. Non-members earn and accumulate points normally but receive no revenue distribution. The $10 annual membership fee is the key that unlocks redistribution eligibility.

The $10 fee becomes a concrete financial proposition: a non-member who has been actively playing has accumulated points that are building in value. Paying $10 unlocks their share of the next distribution. Historical engagement is rewarded — this is the moment the platform's promise to players becomes tangible.

### Points Window

Only points earned in the **trailing 12 months** count toward any distribution. This applies equally to all members. A member who joined recently receives credit only for the 12 months preceding the distribution date. This:

- Prevents disproportionate claims from players who accumulated points over many years before joining
- Keeps all members actively engaged — points older than 12 months carry no distribution value
- Is consistent with the 12-month rolling window used throughout the rest of the design

### Distribution Mechanism

Redistribution is proportional to points earned in the trailing 12 months. The formula is public and the inputs are visible — any member can verify their expected share by dividing their trailing 12-month points by total community trailing 12-month points for the same period.

Distribution is **annual**, following full public financial disclosure. HF publishes complete accounts before any distribution is made — revenue by source, costs by category, profit, and the resulting redistribution amount.

### Cooperative Parallel

This redistribution model is structurally similar to a consumer cooperative participation dividend — members share in surplus proportional to their participation, not their ownership stake. Whether HF should formally incorporate as a cooperative is an open design question with significant legal, tax, and governance implications — see Open Questions Parking Lot.

### Abuse Controls

Redistribution does not begin until robust abuse controls are in place. The primary controls are: membership fee as financial barrier, email verification, account maturity thresholds, and the 12-month points window. Additional anomaly detection is added when redistribution goes live. See Rating Manipulation and Abuse Mitigations for full detail.

### The Three Attack Surfaces

**Attack 1 — Coordinated positive flooding**
A campaign or motivated group creates accounts to flood a candidate or brand with positive surveys, inflating their rating. The system's own mechanics are the natural defence: a successfully inflated rating leads to blacklisting when post-election behaviour diverges. Manipulation that produces a high pre-election rating raises the blacklist bar the manipulator will eventually have to clear.

**Attack 2 — Coordinated negative flooding**
Tanking a candidate's or brand's rating with fake negative surveys. The natural consequence is that the affected candidate or brand has a direct incentive to engage with HF — to request an audit, to endorse, to compete on ethical grounds. Negative manipulation drives the arms race dynamic HF wants to create.

**Attack 3 — Sockpuppet farms**
Creating many accounts to game points accumulation, revenue distribution, or protected action thresholds. This is the attack worth defending against directly.

### Sockpuppet Mitigations

**Primary deterrent — membership fee**
The $10/year membership fee is the primary financial deterrent for revenue distribution abuse. Running 1,000 fake memberships costs $10,000/year. The fee must be paid before meaningful revenue share is earned. This makes sockpuppet farms economically unattractive for all but the most motivated and well-resourced attackers.

**Email verification**
Points do not accrue until email is verified. This raises the cost of throwaway accounts meaningfully — each fake account requires a real, working email address.

**Account maturity threshold**
Protected actions — duplicate flagging, office history edits — require 7 days and 3 survey responses. This makes coordinated attacks on the governance infrastructure more expensive without blocking legitimate users.

**Anomaly detection**
When revenue distribution goes live, accounts that accumulate points in suspicious patterns — unusually fast, in bursts, with identical survey responses — are flagged for review. This is a post-launch addition, not required at Stage 1.

### Infrastructure Security — GCP Cloud Armor

HF uses Google Cloud Armor (Standard tier, ~$8/month) for network-level protection. The following are enabled from day one:

**IP-level rate limiting**
Limits the number of requests — particularly signup and survey submission — from a single IP address in a given period. Stops lazy attacks (bots running from a single server) with essentially zero cost or friction to legitimate users. Does not stop sophisticated attacks using VPN or residential proxies, but raises the cost of casual abuse significantly.

**DDoS protection**
Automatic Layer 3/4 protection built into Cloud Armor. Volumetric attacks are blocked at the network edge before reaching the application.

**OWASP Top 10 WAF rules**
Pre-built rules blocking common web attack patterns: SQL injection, cross-site scripting, path traversal, and others. Maintained by Google. Enabled as a checkbox with no ongoing maintenance required.

**Adaptive Protection**
Cloud Armor's ML-based anomaly detection. Learns normal traffic patterns and automatically flags unusual spikes — particularly relevant for coordinated survey flooding that doesn't originate from a single IP.

**Not enabled at launch:**
Bot management (uses reCAPTCHA, adds user-facing friction — conflicts with low-friction signup philosophy). Enterprise tier ($200/month — overkill at this scale).

### Overview

All three games ask the community to self-manage large amounts of data — stores, products, candidates, jurisdictions, and whatever entities Humanium introduces. Open creation is a core principle: anyone can create anything at any time. But open creation inevitably produces duplicates, errors, and abandoned records. Rather than centralised moderation, HF uses a shared community-governed lifecycle pattern to handle this across all games consistently.

The pattern was first designed for Polium jurisdictions but is general-purpose. It applies to any entity that players create and self-manage.

### The Pattern

**Open creation**
Anyone can create an entity. No verification required. Account maturity is not required to create — only to take actions that affect the structure and integrity of existing data.

**Duplicate prevention UX**
Before creating any entity, the player is shown a fuzzy-matched list of existing entities that closely match what they are entering. Selecting an existing entity is the default path. Creating a new one requires a deliberate additional step. Most duplicates are accidental — good UX prevents the majority before they happen.

**Directional duplicate flagging**
Any sufficiently mature player can flag an entity as a duplicate of another, pointing it toward the entity they consider the legitimate version. Flagging is directional — it is a statement about a specific entity, not a symmetric relationship. Tracked in an intermediate model per entity type with a unique constraint preventing a player from flagging the same entity twice.

Flagging is a **protected action** requiring account maturity: 7 days old and 3 survey responses submitted. This prevents bad actors from triggering the deprecation pipeline against legitimate entities.

**Automatic deprecation**
An entity becomes deprecated when:

> `flag_count ≥ active_engagement / 10`

This threshold recalculates continuously. Active engagement is defined per entity type — for jurisdictions it is followers; for stores it will be players who have purchased there; for candidates it is players who have surveyed or declared. The continuous recalculation means the formula handles both coordinated community flagging and natural decay: an entity whose engagement drops will eventually deprecate itself as the ratio tips, without any new flags being required.

**Manual deprecation**
HF administrators may manually deprecate an entity at community request. This power is exercised only when the community has deliberated in the forum and reached consensus. The admin action is publicly documented with a link to the forum discussion. This is a transparent and legitimate use of administrative authority — never a unilateral editorial decision.

**Automatic deletion**
A deprecated entity with zero active engagement for a configurable period (default: 180 days) is automatically deleted. On deletion:
- Dependents (child entities, followers, associated records) are migrated to the highest-engagement linked entity
- The migration cascades naturally — migrated children go through the same duplicate detection pipeline at their new location
- Affected players are notified with an explanation and a link to the surviving entity
- The deletion is logged permanently for administrative audit purposes

All thresholds are configurable by HF administrators without a code deployment.

### Governance Status

The deprecation formula (`flag_count ≥ active_engagement / 10`) and deletion thresholds are HF policy decisions, not merely technical implementation details. They should be reviewed by the membership and ultimately ratified by the Constitutional Court as the organisation matures. Changes to these thresholds are subject to the same governance process as changes to criteria weights.

### Implementation

The lifecycle pattern is implemented as a shared Django app — `hf/lifecycle/` — containing abstract models and Celery tasks that run deprecation and deletion checks on a scheduled basis. Each game's self-managed entities compose from this shared infrastructure rather than reimplementing it independently.

**First instance:** Polium jurisdictions — see Polium Technical Implementation for the specific implementation.

**Planned instances:** Spendium stores, Spendium products (post-POS integration), Polium candidates (leaf nodes — deletion only, no child migration required).

## Criteria Governance

*This section applies across all HF games — Spendium, Polium, and Humanium.*

Criteria are the yes/no questions that form the basis of all surveys across all games. They are organised into categories. The process for proposing, evaluating, and adopting criteria is a core part of how HF operationalises Article 3 of its constitution (the weight of evidence wins).

### Membership Benefits

Membership costs $10/year. Benefits are active as long as membership is current. They lapse immediately if membership is not renewed and resume on renewal.

**All members receive:**

- **Points multiplier** — 1.5x points on all actions while membership is current. Drops to 1x if membership lapses. Same multiplier for all members regardless of when they joined.
- **Founding member badge** — members who join during Phase 1 (before 100 members) receive a permanent founding member designation on their profile and public survey responses. This designation cannot be earned at any later stage. It is a historical marker, not a tiered privilege.
- **Member badge** — visible on profile and public survey responses for all members at all phases.
- **Members-only forum category** — a Discourse category visible to all but postable only by members. The founder engages here directly. Players can read but not post. A privileged first-mover voice in governance deliberation before formal voting rights exist.
- **Governance rights** — from Phase 2 onward, members can propose criteria, vote on proposals, and refer adopted criteria to the Constitutional Court. The most significant benefit and the reason membership exists.

### Phase 1: Founder with Public Record (Now → First 100 Members)

The founder sets all criteria, categories, and weights. Every decision is documented publicly in the Discourse forum with explicit reasoning — why this criterion was chosen, what evidence supports it, what weight it carries and why. Nothing is hidden.

Any player can propose a criterion in the forum at any time. The founder responds publicly to every proposal — accepting, rejecting, or deferring with reasoning. This is not democracy yet, but it is accountability.

Members during Phase 1 receive all membership benefits above. Governance voting rights activate at Phase 2.

This phase creates a public record the community can critique and a precedent of transparency that becomes constitutional expectation.

### Phase 2: Member Advisory Process (First 100 Members → First 500 Members)

Criteria changes require a documented community deliberation period before the founder decides.

1. **Proposal** — any member submits a criterion proposal to the forum. Must include: the yes/no question, the category it belongs to, the proposed weight, and the evidence or reasoning connecting it to commons impact.
2. **Discussion period** — 30 days of open forum discussion. Any player can comment. Any member can formally second or oppose.
3. **Member signal** — members vote at the end of the discussion period. Advisory, not binding. The founder retains final say but must respond publicly if overriding a community recommendation, citing a specific constitutional article as justification.
4. **Founder decision** — documented publicly. Rejecting a well-evidenced community recommendation without constitutional grounds would itself be a constitutional issue under Article 3.

### Phase 3: Member Governance (500+ Members)

The founder's veto is removed. Criteria changes are determined entirely by the member community.

1. **Proposal** — any member submits to the forum with: question, category, proposed weight, and evidence of commons impact.
2. **Discussion period** — 30 days open to all players. Members may formally second or oppose.
3. **Member vote** — binding. Requires **60% supermajority** of participating members. Minimum participation threshold of **10% of active members** required — prevents small groups passing changes quietly during low-engagement periods.
4. **Constitutional Court review** — any member in good standing may refer a passed criterion to the Constitutional Court within **14 days** of adoption, on grounds that it violates a constitutional article. The Court must respond within **30 days** of referral. Failure to respond is treated as no constitutional objection and the criterion stands.
5. **Adoption** — criterion becomes active if not struck down by the Court within the review window.

### Member in Good Standing

A member in good standing is one whose:
- Annual membership fee is current and not lapsed
- Account has not been suspended by HF administrators
- Account meets the standard maturity threshold (7 days old, 3 survey responses submitted)

### Criterion Lifecycle

**States:** Proposed → Under Discussion → Voted → Active → Under Review → Retired

**Trial period** — newly adopted criteria are flagged as "trial" for the first 6 months. Active and affecting ratings during this period, but automatically reviewed at the end of the trial. The community votes to confirm or retire based on real data.

**Retirement** — any member can propose retiring an existing criterion. Same process as adoption. A criterion that fails to receive a renewal confirmation vote after **3 years** is automatically retired.

### Weights Governance

Weights follow the same proposal, discussion, and voting process as criteria. Any weight change must be accompanied by evidence of why the proposed weight better reflects commons impact than the current one. Weight changes without evidentiary justification are constitutionally suspect under Article 3.

### Phase Thresholds

The 100-member and 500-member thresholds are defaults. Quality of deliberation matters more than count. These thresholds are configurable and subject to review by the Constitutional Court as the organisation matures.

## Player Signup Experience

### Design Philosophy

The signup experience must be as frictionless as possible. Every additional field, step, or decision is a barrier between a potential player and HF. Research consistently shows that reducing form fields and offering modern authentication methods dramatically improves conversion. HF asks for nothing it does not immediately need.

### Authentication Options

Players choose from four paths at signup. All lead to the same account. A player can add additional methods later.

**Google One-tap / Sign in with Apple**
One tap, no form, no password. Name and email come from the existing account. The lowest friction path — prominently featured as the default. No Google or Apple account required to use HF; these are optional convenience paths.

**Magic link**
Player enters their email address only. HF sends a one-click login link. No password, no username, no further steps. Works on any device. Ideal for shared or public devices since it leaves no credentials behind.

**Passkey**
Player registers a passkey tied to their device biometric — Face ID, fingerprint, Windows Hello. After initial setup, login is a single touch or glance. No password, no email required for returning logins, no third-party account involved. The most secure and frictionless option for returning users on personal devices. Passkey support is now excellent across iOS, Android, Mac, and Windows.

No password creation is required or offered at signup. Passwords are the primary source of both friction and security problems. Players who register via magic link or social login can add a passkey later for faster subsequent logins.

### What We Ask For At Signup

Three fields maximum, two of which may be pre-filled:

- **Display name** — one field, whatever the player wants to be called. Pre-filled from social login if available. Display names are **not globally unique** — two players can share the same display name. Public-facing profiles display the name alongside a short SQID fragment as a disambiguator (e.g. *John Smith #a3kx*), making every player uniquely identifiable without forcing awkward username choices at signup. Players can change their display name at any time without breaking anything.
- **Email address** — required for magic link; optional if using social login or passkey. Pre-filled from social login if available.
- **Jurisdiction** — country and region only, presented as two dropdowns pre-populated by device locale as a sensible default. Used to surface relevant elections and stores immediately.

Nothing else. No date of birth, no phone number, no password, no "confirm password", no CAPTCHA.

### Progressive Profiling

Everything beyond the minimum is collected when it becomes relevant. The first time a player tries to follow a specific local election, we ask for their city. Profile photo, bio, and additional jurisdiction preferences are optional and prompted contextually. We never ask for things before they are needed.

### Anonymous Browsing

Anyone can browse ratings, candidate profiles, brand profiles, and survey results without creating an account. Signup is required only to take actions — submitting a survey, declaring a vote, logging a purchase, earning points. This removes all barriers to discovery and lets HF's content do the recruiting.

### Email Verification

Email verification gates points accrual, not access. The flow is deliberately soft — players are never blocked from the app while waiting to verify.

**On signup:** The player is logged in immediately. A verification email is sent in the background. They land directly in the app, ready to play.

**In the app:** A gentle, persistent but non-intrusive banner: *"Verify your email to start earning points. Check your inbox for a link from Human Flourishing."* The banner stays until verification is complete. It does not block anything or interrupt gameplay.

**On clicking the verification link:** Email is marked verified. Points begin accruing immediately. The banner disappears. The link works on any device, including a different one from where the player signed up.

**If they never verify:** The player can browse, survey, and engage indefinitely — they simply never earn points. A gentle reminder is sent once a week for the first month. After that, no further reminders. No nagging.

**Magic link users:** Clicking the login link is de facto email verification — a working email was required to receive it. Magic link users are considered verified on first login and see no verification banner.

**Google and Apple users:** Considered verified immediately — their email was already verified by the provider.

**Passkey users without email:** The only path that requires a separate verification step. The banner and reminder flow applies to these users.

### Member Upgrade

When a player chooses to become a member and pay the $10/year fee, that is the natural point for a slightly higher identity bar — verified email (already required for points) and payment details. Nothing in the signup flow anticipates or front-loads the membership upgrade. Players discover the value of membership through using the game, not through being upsold at the door.

### Protection Without Friction

- No CAPTCHA at signup — annoying and largely ineffective against determined bots
- Rate limiting on signup attempts per IP — invisible to legitimate users
- Email verification gates points accrual, not access
- Account maturity for protected actions is time and behaviour based — no additional signup steps required

## Evidence

*Applies across all HF games — Spendium, Polium, and Humanium.*

### Purpose

Evidence exists to inform surveys and is the primary audit mechanism of HF. It is the research layer that sits beneath the rating layer. A player who is about to survey a candidate or brand can browse the evidence on that subject's profile to make a more informed assessment. Evidence does not directly affect a rating — only survey responses do. But good evidence generates better surveys, and better surveys generate more accurate ratings.

The connection is: **evidence informs surveys, surveys produce ratings, ratings drive behaviour.**

The community is the audit team. An army of engaged players with mobile phones, each contributing what they know and can verify, produces a more comprehensive and harder-to-game picture of a subject's ethics than any single formal audit process. Corporate audits serve a specialist function on top of this community layer — they do not replace it.

### The Four-Tier Evidence Architecture

Evidence exists in four tiers, reflecting the spectrum from fully public to fully sealed. Each tier does what it is best suited for.

---

**Tier 1 — Open Community Evidence**

Publicly submitted by any player. Publicly visible on the subject's profile. Crowd-evaluated through the usefulness voting and flagging system. This is the primary and most powerful evidence layer — broad, scalable, and impossible for any single actor to control or suppress.

Anyone can submit. The community evaluates quality. The best evidence rises to the top.

---

**Tier 2 — Protected Whistleblower Evidence**

Submitted by players with insider knowledge — employees, contractors, suppliers, or others with access to information that is not publicly available. The submitter's identity is stripped by HF at submission and is never stored in association with the evidence. Metadata that could identify the source is removed.

The evidence is publicly visible on the subject's profile, evaluated by the community in the same way as Tier 1 evidence. The protection is in the anonymisation, not in the restriction of the evidence itself.

This is the mechanism by which people who know where the bodies are buried can contribute that knowledge to the public record without fear of retaliation. It is how Article 3 (the weight of evidence wins) gets its teeth when corporations control the evidence.

HF has a legal obligation not to disclose whistleblower identities. The whistleblower provision and the legal framework protecting it are an open design question requiring specialist legal input — see Open Questions Parking Lot.

---

**Tier 3 — Specialist Corporate Audit Evidence**

Produced by domain experts contracted by HF to verify specific Tier 2b criteria — those requiring technical expertise that the crowd cannot provide. Examples: carbon accounting methodology, supply chain certification validity, wage disclosure accuracy, financial statement integrity.

The scope of corporate audits is deliberately narrow: HF does not audit whether a brand behaves ethically in general — the crowd does that. HF audits whether specific technical claims a brand makes are verifiable and accurate. This makes the audit harder to game and more credible.

The audit result — a yes or no verdict on each audited criterion — is published publicly and locked for the duration of the audit window. The underlying data provided by the corporation may be subject to a confidentiality agreement and is not necessarily published. The finding is what is public, not the evidence base.

Corporations requesting audits must agree in advance that the result will be published regardless of outcome. A corporation cannot suppress a negative audit result.

---

**Tier 4 — Sealed Evidence**

Evidence that is too sensitive for public release — because innocent individuals are identifiable within it, because the nature of the evidence would make the source obvious even if anonymised, or because public disclosure would cause harm disproportionate to the public interest served.

Sealed evidence is held securely by HF. It is never published. Access is restricted to:

- The Constitutional Court, when adjudicating a specific case that requires it
- A designated panel of trusted reviewers — senior members in good standing or contracted specialists — operating under formal confidentiality agreements
- Law enforcement, under legal compulsion only

The public-facing output of sealed evidence is a **finding** — a statement that HF has reviewed sealed evidence and that it supports or does not support a specific criterion assessment. The finding is published. The evidence is not. The corporation or candidate subject to a sealed evidence finding cannot challenge it by demanding to see the underlying material, because the sealed process is itself constitutionally legitimate under Article 3.

This tier makes HF a trusted third party with legal obligations to both the submitter and the subject of the evidence. The legal and liability implications of this role are significant and require specialist legal design before this tier is operational — see Open Questions Parking Lot.

---

### Criteria Tiers and Auditability

Not all criteria can be audited. Before a criterion can be adopted, the community must be able to answer: how would this be verified? This creates a two-tier criteria system:

**Survey-only criteria** — yes/no questions the community can have informed opinions about but that cannot be objectively verified. Valuable community signal. Not auditable. Example: "Does this brand feel like it genuinely cares about its workers?"

**Auditable criteria** — yes/no questions with a defined, objective verification method. These divide further:

- **Tier 2a — Publicly auditable** — verifiable from public records alone. No corporate cooperation required. Example: voting records, public environmental filings, published pay ratios.
- **Tier 2b — Privately auditable** — requires access to non-public information under confidentiality. Example: internal supply chain ethics, proprietary environmental data, internal wage structures.

The auditability tier of each criterion is documented publicly alongside its weight.

### Where Evidence Appears

Evidence appears on the profile page of the subject it relates to — a candidate's profile in Polium, a brand's profile in Spendium. Tiers 1 and 2 are visible to any visitor. Tier 3 audit verdicts are displayed prominently. Tier 4 findings are displayed as attributed statements from HF.

### What a Piece of Evidence Contains (Tiers 1 and 2)

- A URL pointing to the source (Tier 1) or a description of the evidence (Tier 2, no URL that could identify the source)
- A short note explaining what the evidence shows and why it is relevant to HF criteria
- An optional link to a specific criterion it is most relevant to
- The submitting player's display name and SQID fragment (Tier 1 only — Tier 2 is anonymous)
- A timestamp

### Evidence Quality and Lifecycle (Tiers 1 and 2)

Evidence is ranked by net usefulness score. Misleading or malicious evidence can be flagged by sufficiently mature accounts. When flag count exceeds the threshold relative to usefulness score, evidence is hidden. HF administrators can permanently remove genuinely harmful content. Evidence is never deleted otherwise.

### What Evidence Is Not

Evidence is not a comment system. Evidence is not a rating mechanism — submitting evidence earns no points and does not change a subject's rating directly. Evidence is not curated or verified by HF at Tiers 1 and 2 — its quality is self-evident and community-evaluated.

### Evidence Tier Rollout

The four evidence tiers are rolled out incrementally. Building trust and legal capability before adding complexity is more important than launching all tiers simultaneously.

**Launch — Tier 1: Open Community Evidence**
Public URL submissions with notes, usefulness voting, and flagging. No legal complexity, no identity management, no specialist auditors. Already fully designed. Ships with Polium at launch.

**Stage 2 — Tier 2: Protected Whistleblower Evidence**
Anonymous submission channel with identity stripping and metadata removal. Requires specialist legal input to define non-disclosure obligations and procedural protections before going live. Should launch after Tier 1 has demonstrated that the evidence system is genuinely used and valued — credibility first.

**Stage 3 — Tier 3: Specialist Corporate Audits**
The paid audit product. Requires auditor qualification process, methodology publication, pre-audit disclosure workflow, and confidentiality agreements with corporations. Corporations will not pay for an audit from an organisation with no track record — Tiers 1 and 2 build that track record. Maps to the Spendium build stage of the roadmap.

**Stage 4 — Tier 4: Sealed Evidence**
The most legally complex tier. Requires trusted reviewer panel, sealed storage infrastructure, Constitutional Court procedures, and a full legal framework for HF acting as a trusted third party. Only built when there is a demonstrated need and the organisation has the legal and governance maturity to handle it responsibly. Not built speculatively.

### Open Design Questions

- **Whistleblower legal framework** — the legal obligations, liability protections, and procedural requirements for operating a whistleblower submission channel; requires specialist legal input before Tier 2 is operational
- **Sealed evidence legal framework** — the legal obligations and liability implications of HF acting as a trusted third party holding sensitive evidence; requires specialist legal input before Tier 4 is operational
- **Trusted reviewer panel** — composition, appointment, confidentiality obligations, and decision-making process for the panel that reviews Tier 4 evidence
- **Auditor qualification** — how HF auditors for Tier 3 are identified, qualified, contracted, and held accountable

## Survey Mechanics

*This section applies across all HF games — Spendium, Polium, and Humanium.*

### One Survey Per Player Per Subject

Every player has at most one active survey response per subject — a candidate, a store, or a product. Submitting a new survey on a subject you have already surveyed replaces your previous response entirely. One player, one current opinion. The old response is removed from the rating calculation immediately.

### Re-Survey Cool-Down

A player must wait a minimum period before updating their survey of a subject. The default is **30 days**. Configurable by HF administrators without a code deployment.

### Expiry Refresh on Update

When a player updates their survey, the 12-month expiry clock resets from the date of the update. A survey submitted 11 months ago and updated today will not expire for another 12 months. This ensures that engaged players who maintain their assessments contribute to ratings continuously.

### Survey Points

All point values are configurable by HF administrators without a code deployment.

- **First survey on a subject:** 100 points
- **Second survey on the same subject:** 50 points
- **Third and all subsequent surveys:** 25 points permanently

The permanent floor ensures that engaged players who maintain their assessments over years continue to earn points and matter in revenue distribution. It rewards sustained engagement without making unlimited re-surveying as valuable as surveying new subjects. The 30-day cool-down means the maximum earning rate from re-surveying a single subject is approximately 300 points per year — meaningful for genuinely engaged players, not worth gaming.

### Survey Visibility

By default, survey responses are private. A player may toggle any individual survey as publicly visible. When toggled public:

- The player's display name and SQID fragment (e.g. *John Smith #a3kx*) and full response detail are shown on the subject's profile page under "What players are saying"
- The player may optionally add a short note of up to **200 characters** explaining their overall reasoning for the survey
- The player is offered the option to share the survey to social media via the in-app share button, with a pre-populated post linking to the subject's HF profile

Sharing a survey to social media earns an additional points multiplier — consistent with the vote declaration social share mechanic in Polium. Supported platforms at launch: **Instagram** and **TikTok**. Additional platforms to be added over time.

A respected player's public endorsement or criticism carries real weight and serves the ethical arms race dynamic.

### Where Surveys Are Tracked

- **Player profile page** — a complete list of all subjects the player has surveyed, showing their current response, optional note, date of last update, points earned, and a link to each subject's profile. Makes it easy for players to revisit assessments when new information arrives.
- **Subject profile page** — aggregate rating and survey count always visible. Individually attributed responses shown only when the player has toggled them public, displaying username, full response detail, and optional note.

Social media sharing of surveys follows the shared Social Media Share Mechanics — see that section for points, frequency, content, platforms, and verification rules.

## Social Media Share Mechanics

*Applies across all HF games.*

### Shareable Actions

Any significant player action can be shared to social media:
- Submitting a survey (any game)
- Updating a survey with new information
- Declaring a vote in Polium
- Logging a purchase in Spendium

### Points

Sharing earns a **flat bonus of 50 points** per share event, added on top of the base action points. Flat rather than a multiplier so that all shares are rewarded equally regardless of the subject's rating. Configurable by HF administrators without a code deployment.

### Share Frequency

Points are earned once per action instance. When a player submits a survey, they may share it and earn the bonus. When they later update that survey — a new action — they may share again and earn again. Sharing the same action event twice earns no additional points. This rewards genuine ongoing engagement without enabling farming.

### Share Content

Each share generates a pre-designed card image containing:
- The subject's name and type (candidate, brand, product)
- The subject's current HF rating, displayed visually
- The player's optional 200-character note
- The HF logo and a link to the subject's profile page

The player can edit their note before posting. The HF link, subject name, and rating are locked. The card is rendered server-side from a fixed template with dynamic fields — no external design tools required. The card format is appropriate for both stories and feed posts on image-first platforms.

### Platforms at Launch

Instagram and TikTok. Additional platforms added over time.

### Verification

Trust-based. The player taps the share button in the app, the share is initiated, and points are awarded immediately. HF records that the share was initiated but does not verify that the post actually appeared on the platform. Consistent with the overall philosophy of rewarding engagement over auditing it.

## Community Strategy

### Why Community Matters

Both games will naturally generate communities around them — players discussing candidates, debating store ethics, proposing criteria, and organising around shared values. HF does not need to control this and should not try to. The goal is to ensure that:

- There is a canonical place where discussions that matter are findable
- HF has a clear voice in those discussions
- The community does not fracture across multiple platforms at launch
- Structured deliberation — criteria proposals, governance debates, weight discussions — happens in a place designed for it

The forum is not just a community tool. It is a governance institution. Criteria proposals, weight debates, and constitutional questions should be discussed and deliberated in the forum before going to the membership for decision. This should be designed for from the start, not retrofitted later.

### Primary Platform: Discourse

Discourse is the chosen forum platform. It is open source, self-hostable, searchable, threaded, and purpose-built for the kind of deliberative discussion HF needs. It is significantly better than Discord for structured governance discussion — Discord loses important conversations in scrollback within hours.

Discourse supports **DiscourseConnect** (SSO) — when implemented, a player's HF account is their forum account. One login, one identity. Membership status, points total, and governance role can be surfaced in the forum automatically.

### Hosting Strategy

**Option 1 — Discourse Open Source Free Hosting (preferred)**
Discourse offers Pro-level hosted plans at no cost for qualifying open source projects. HF is AGPL v3 licensed with a genuine civic mission — it is a strong candidate. The Pro plan includes DiscourseConnect SSO, custom domain, and full features at zero cost.

**Immediate action:** apply for the Discourse open source hosting programme before building anything else. Application takes approximately 20 minutes.

**Option 2 — Discourse Free Hosted Tier (fallback)**
If the open source application is unsuccessful, start on Discourse's free hosted tier. This uses Discourse ID for login rather than SSO — players have a separate forum account from their HF account. This is a known limitation and is acceptable at Stage 1 when the player population is small.

The migration path from free hosted to self-hosted (with full SSO) is clean and well-documented — Discourse supports backup and restore between hosting environments, and posts migrate with user accounts when SSO is linked. Early community members will need to link their forum account to their HF account during migration, which is a minor friction point worth communicating clearly when it happens.

**Option 3 — Self-Hosted (future)**
When SSO integration with the HF Django application becomes a priority — or when the open source hosting programme is unavailable and the free tier is insufficient — self-hosting Discourse on AWS is the natural path. Discourse runs comfortably on a small EC2 instance (~$20/month) and fits naturally into the existing AWS stack. DiscourseConnect SSO is available on self-hosted instances at no additional cost.

### Discord

A Discord server should be added when there are enough early users that real-time community warmth becomes valuable — roughly around Stage 2 of the roadmap. Starting with Discord before there is a player base will result in an empty server that signals lack of traction. Discord is a complement to Discourse, not a replacement — it handles real-time conversation while Discourse handles structured deliberation.

### Reddit

A subreddit will likely form whether HF creates one or not. HF should claim the official subreddit early to establish a legitimate presence, then use it to point substantive discussions toward Discourse. Reddit should not be depended upon as a primary community platform.

### SSO Design Note

When DiscourseConnect SSO is implemented, the Django view should pass the following fields to Discourse:

- Player SQID as the external user ID
- Username and email from the HF account
- Membership status (member / player) — maps to a Discourse group or trust level
- Whether the player is a founder or Constitutional Court member — maps to Discourse moderator or admin status

This makes forum identity and governance role visible in every post, without requiring separate forum administration.
